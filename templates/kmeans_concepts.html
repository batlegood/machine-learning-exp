K-Means: Concepts

Unsupervised Learning

Definition

K-Means is an unsupervised learning algorithm used to group data into a fixed number of groups called clusters. Unlike supervised methods, there are no prior labels: the algorithm discovers patterns and similarities in the data by itself. Each cluster is represented by a centroid, which is the mean point of the records assigned to that group. The goal of K-Means is to minimize the distance between the records and their centroid, achieving groups that are as compact and distinct as possible.

Clustering

Clustering is a segmentation technique that divides a dataset into homogeneous groups. The idea is that elements within the same cluster share similar characteristics, while elements from different clusters are distinct from each other. This approach is applied in multiple fields: customer segmentation in marketing, document grouping in search engines, image classification, user behavior analysis, among others. In the case of students, clustering allows identifying academic profiles based on their study hours and grades.

Operation of K-Means

The algorithm follows an iterative process that ensures clusters progressively adapt to the actual structure of the data:

  1. Initialization: k initial centroids are chosen randomly.
  2. Assignment: each record is assigned to the cluster whose centroid is closest, calculating the Euclidean distance.
  3. Update: centroids are recalculated as the mean of the records assigned to each cluster.
  4. Iteration: the steps of assignment and update are repeated until the centroids stop moving significantly or the maximum number of iterations is reached.

This cycle ensures that the final clusters are consistent and reflect real patterns in the data.

Role of Centroids

Centroids are the heart of the algorithm. They represent the "center" of each cluster and determine how records are grouped. At the beginning, centroids may be far from the data, but with each iteration they adjust until they reflect the average position of the assigned records. In practical terms, centroids are the coordinates that summarize the typical behavior of each group. For example, a centroid may represent students with few study hours and low grades, while another may represent students with many study hours and high grades.

Iterations

Each iteration is a complete cycle of assignment and update. In the first iteration, the initial centroids are often not representative, but after several iterations the centroids converge to stable positions. The algorithm stops when the centroids no longer change significantly or when the maximum number of iterations is reached. This process ensures that the final clusters are consistent and reflect real patterns in the data.
